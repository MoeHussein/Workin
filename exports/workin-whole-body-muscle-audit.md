## Scope and terminology

The human body contains skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscle. A voluntary workout
program primarily targets skeletal muscle. Cardiac muscle is trained indirectly
through aerobic conditioning, while smooth muscle is not programmed with
resistance exercises.

There is no single useful count of “all muscles” for workout design because
anatomical references may count bilateral muscles, separate heads, serial
segmental muscles, variable muscles, and small intrinsic muscles differently.
This audit therefore:

1. accounts for every major anatomical region;
2. names all important locomotor and postural skeletal muscles individually or
   in their accepted anatomical groups;
3. groups very small serial or intrinsic muscles when they share the same
   training conclusion; and
4. distinguishes a true program omission from a muscle that does not need
   isolation in a general fitness plan.

Coverage labels:

- **Direct:** a principal intended target of a prescribed resistance exercise.
- **Strong indirect:** a substantial synergist or stabilizer during hard sets.
- **Light/incidental:** active during posture, walking, breathing, balance, or
  stabilization but not receiving a clear progressive resistance dose.
- **Absent:** no meaningful progressive stimulus.
- **Not a routine target:** anatomically present but not sensibly isolated in a
  general workout without a specific clinical, occupational, or sport reason.

“Lower abs” are not a separate muscle. The upper and lower visible sections belong
to the same rectus abdominis. Exercise selection can emphasize different joint
actions and regions, but lower-abdominal visibility also depends strongly on
body-fat distribution and anatomy.

## Overall coverage verdict

### Well covered

- Latissimus dorsi and upper back
- Chest
- Anterior and posterior shoulders
- Biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis through compound pulling
- Triceps through compound pressing
- Quadriceps
- Gluteus maximus
- Trunk anti-extension and lateral stability
- Grip and finger flexors

### Present but comparatively underemphasized

- Hip-extension hamstrings
- Calves and soleus
- Gluteus medius/minimus
- Rotator cuff external rotation
- Spinal extensors, depending on actual RDL resistance
- Dynamic abdominal flexion
- Vertical pressing

### Meaningful gaps for this trainee’s stated goals

1. **Lateral deltoids:** no progressive shoulder-abduction work.
2. **Hamstring knee flexion, especially biceps femoris short head:** no leg curl.
3. **Dynamic abdominal work:** current abdominal training is almost entirely
   isometric.
4. **Dip skill:** not a missing muscle group, but a missing calisthenics movement
   now that stable dip bars are confirmed.
5. **Tibialis anterior:** no direct dorsiflexion work.

### Optional specialization, not fundamental defects

- Direct biceps curls
- Direct triceps extensions
- Direct neck work
- Loaded hip abduction/adduction
- Dedicated external rotation
- Forearm extensor or finger-extension work
- Intrinsic foot work

## Implemented minimum-change correction

The current program already contains substantial workload. The efficient
correction is to replace redundant patterns before adding many sets:

1. **Replace Friday push-ups with assisted parallel-bar dips, 3 × 5–10.**
   Assistance keeps the movement inside the prescribed range while the clean,
   pain-free dip baseline is established. This develops dip skill without
   stacking another pressing exercise on top.
2. **Replace Monday plank/hollow choice with reverse crunches, 2 × 10–15.**
   Keep Friday hollow holds and Wednesday side planks.
3. **Replace Wednesday glute bridges with standing band hamstring curls,
   2 × 10–15 per side.** Gluteus maximus already receives substantial squat,
   split-squat, and RDL work; this adds the missing knee-flexion function.
4. **Add band lateral raises, 2 × 12–20, once weekly** on Strength A.
5. **Add tibialis raises, 2 × 15–25, once weekly** beside Friday calf raises.
6. **Add band curls, 2 × 8–15, once weekly** on Strength B to provide a small
   direct biceps dose while compound pulling remains primary.
7. **Add no-anchor band external rotation, 2 × 12–20, on Tuesday** as
   low-fatigue shoulder-control work.
8. **Add band lateral walks, 2 × 10–15 steps per side, on Saturday** as
   low-fatigue hip-abductor work.

These changes address the most meaningful uncovered areas while retaining three
primary strength days. Tuesday and Saturday additions are deliberately
low-fatigue, Thursday remains recovery, and Sunday remains full rest. A separate
triceps extension was not added because the new dips plus pike push-ups and
push-ups already provide substantial elbow-extension work; adding it now would
duplicate pressing volume rather than correct a coverage gap.
